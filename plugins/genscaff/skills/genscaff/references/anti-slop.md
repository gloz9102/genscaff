# Product-Grounded Anti-Slop Prevention and Review

Use when routed by the main Skill, before composition and copy decisions and again against the implemented result. Apply only the authorized scope: Quick reviews its affected component/path; Standard covers the changed surfaces and primary flow. Reuse the product/design contract and existing findings rather than creating a second specification or mandatory report.

## What to diagnose

Treat slop as an operational concern: apparently finished UI whose choices lack product context, useful information relationships, coherent expression, or complete behavior. This is a review framework, not a standardized psychological construct or a calibrated score.

Keep three judgments separate:

| Dimension | Required observation | Evidence limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visual sameness | Unsupported repetition, interchangeable composition, or conflicting visual rules | A capture can show composition; it cannot establish authorship or working behavior. |
| Product specificity | Recognizable domain objects, useful information, necessary decisions, truthful claims, and task-appropriate emphasis | A name, slogan, palette, unusual font, or report description does not establish specificity. |
| Experience completeness | Actions produce their represented outcomes, state is understandable, recovery is available where real, and relevant users can operate the flow | Exercise behavior; source code, screenshots, and positive aesthetic comments alone are insufficient. |

A conventional interface may be specific and complete; an unusual interface may still be hollow or unusable. Never infer AI use, low effort, or creator intent from appearance. Do not collapse these dimensions into an aggregate slop score, CSS blacklist, originality claim, or universal clean boolean.

## Decision rule

Follow the main Skill's priority: explicit requirements and locks, existing product/design system, accessibility and functional integrity, archetype/surface, then craft heuristics. Record conflicts rather than silently overriding a higher-priority requirement.

Judge a finding by an observable mismatch with a task, required fact, information relationship, interaction expectation, or established visual rule. A single unsupported factual claim, misleading action, obscured critical fact, or inaccessible control is actionable. Do not wait for a second design domain or a minimum number of symptoms.

Repeated unsupported defaults across composition, content, geometry, imagery, and motion strengthen a visual-sameness diagnosis. Inspect visible decorative eyebrows, section numbers, repeated cards, and nested containers, but do not fail any merely for existing. Specify what meaning or task they obscure or fail to support. Where the only objection is personal taste, identify it as preference and preserve the current direction unless a change was requested.

Preserve purposeful gradients, glass, rounding, centered layouts, common fonts, component libraries, familiar navigation, and necessary dashboard density. Their presence does not establish a defect. Substituting oversized serif type, texture, asymmetry, brutalism, elaborate motion, or a different palette is not a correction unless it resolves the diagnosed problem and fits the product.

## Prevent before implementation

Use real or clearly identified representative content while shaping the layout. Record the following decisions in the existing contract; a Quick task needs only the applicable local decisions:

- Who uses this surface, in what context, to read, decide, create, compare, monitor, or complete which job? Identify the observable result. Reading, media discovery, and brand experience are legitimate jobs; do not manufacture a transaction or recovery flow for them.
- Which user questions and constraints must be answered at this stage? Identify required facts, units, conditions, evidence, and input/output boundaries. Distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions and unknowns; request essential missing information without inventing it.
- What should be noticed first, next, and at the moment of action? Tie emphasis to urgency, consequence, and the task rather than decorative size or component convenience.
- Which relationships must remain visible: comparison across shared attributes, temporal order, parent/child grouping, operational status, or narrative progression? Choose a structure that exposes them.
- Which visual choices express this product, and which existing conventions should remain familiar? State their roles in hierarchy, recognition, tone, or understanding; novelty itself is not a rationale.
- Which content and state variations could break the changed surface? Identify relevant long/localized text, missing facts or media, data volume, permissions, delays, failure, cancellation, persistence, and recovery before styling only the happy path.

Give each proposed section a user question, domain object, decision, action, explanation, or evidenced expressive role. Change its structure or omit it if no role exists, preserving all required information. Do not fill a predetermined hero/features/statistics/testimonials/CTA recipe or add content to occupy whitespace.

## Review every applicable dimension

For Standard, account for every row below as supported by an observation, an identified finding, a scoped unverified limitation, or a concrete inapplicability reason. Quick applies only the affected rows. These are dimensions to inspect, not mandatory UI features or a new boolean form.

| Dimension | Inspect | Correction target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product context | Could distant products reuse the same content, data, controls, and composition after superficial relabeling? | Show real domain attributes, recognizable vocabulary, constraints, decisions, and outcomes. Shared navigation and accessible primitives may remain generic. |
| Task hierarchy | Do urgent work, decision-critical facts, and the next useful action get appropriate emphasis? Are decorative metrics or equally loud panels displacing them? | Make visual order match the task; keep related decision information together and secondary detail reachable. A large title alone is not hierarchy. |
| Information relationships | Do cards hide comparisons, equal tiles flatten sequence or urgency, or nested borders separate facts that belong together? | Select tables/comparison rows for shared attributes, ordered steps for sequence, lists/queues for scanning, cards for independent items, and narrative/media for explanation as appropriate. Preserve useful grouping. |
| Copy and information value | Can users identify the subject, action, result, relevant conditions, and next step? Are slogans, redundant helper text, arbitrary numbers, or identical section introductions substituting for facts? | Replace vague uplift with truthful product facts and action-specific language; retain a headline's expressive role when nearby content supplies necessary meaning. Do not invent capabilities to make copy concrete. |
| Proof and data | Are claims, reviews, logos, availability, statuses, and charts supported? Do displayed values agree, and are units, period, comparison basis, and sample status clear where needed? | Use traceable evidence or visibly disclosed sample/concept material. Remove unsupported claims without removing required facts. A smooth graph, precise number, or stock image is not evidence. |
| Action continuity | Do labels predict destinations/results, prerequisites appear before commitment, feedback follow activation, and actual state/data match the claimed completion? | Complete the applicable cue-to-result path, including secondary controls introduced within scope. Verify generated output, selected/filtered content, persisted changes, or real destination as promised. A toast alone is insufficient. |
| State completeness | Does the design distinguish initial emptiness, zero search matches, loading, missing data, error, permission limits, and completion where these can occur? | Supply accurate state text, retained input, useful next actions, and real retry/edit/cancel/back paths. Check relevant long strings, zero/one/many items, large values, and interrupted/failed operations. Never replace unknown values with misleading zeroes or fabricate impossible states. |
| Visual coherence | Do type roles, Korean fallback fonts, line breaks, icon weight, spacing, alignment, geometry, surfaces, and semantic colors form one consistent system? | Reuse role-based tokens and primitives; repair rendered mismatches. Preserve natural Korean phrases, legible secondary text, comparable numeric columns, and safe wrapping. Do not redesign merely to satisfy arbitrary font/weight/radius counts. |
| Media and motion | Do media explain the product, subject, process, result, or intended atmosphere? Does motion communicate feedback, state, continuity, or an evidenced expressive purpose? | Keep useful assets and meaningful crops. Remove unrelated ornament or intrusive animation; preserve immediate actions, stable targets, readable static/reduced-motion paths, and useful media-failure behavior. |
| Responsive and accessible use | Do meaningful order, required facts, action access, keyboard/focus, contrast, names, and applicable status announcements survive narrow layouts and content changes? | Recompose by priority and repair actual access barriers; decorative polish does not offset them. Distinguish screenshots, keyboard tests, automation, and assistive-technology evidence. |

Use substitution as a diagnostic prompt, not a required count of broken structural axes. Familiar login, search, table, and checkout conventions should not be made unfamiliar to pass it. Product specificity must come from the relevant content and behavior; do not add meaningless objects or fake decisions to meet a signal quota.

## Concrete calibration examples

- An expense dashboard gives lifetime totals most of the viewport while rejected submissions and missing receipts are hard to find. Reprioritize the review queue and its reasons/actions; keep useful summary metrics secondary. Changing the accent does not fix the hierarchy.
- A plan comparison gives every plan an independent card with facts in different orders. Align shared attributes and expose relevant conditions at the decision point. Cards remain valid if they support the actual comparison.
- A report action shows a success toast but creates no report. Implement the promised artifact/result or disclose the prototype boundary at the control. A polished toast does not close this defect.
- Search renders the same empty message before any records exist and after restrictive filters hide records. Distinguish the causes and offer the applicable create, clear, or revise path while preserving the query and inputs.
- A familiar gray interface uses realistic domain data, coherent hierarchy, and complete keyboard behavior. Preserve it when it meets the brief; unfamiliar fonts and asymmetry are not required improvements.
- An editorial page has readable structure and citations but no mutation. Preserve its reading/navigation job; omit invented loading, confirmation, and undo flows.

## Evidence and correction

For Standard, inspect the first selected-product desktop/mobile captures with actual content and walk the affected primary flow and relevant transitions. Quick retains its affected-path checks and representative-viewport requirement; this reference does not add a broad contract or full Standard walkthrough. Judge exercised behavior separately from screenshots. A static visual finding can be recorded immediately; mark unexercised behavior unverified.

For each finding record, in the existing contract/issues:

`dimension -> location/state/viewport -> observation and artifact -> affected user task or requirement -> response and change -> recheck result or remaining limitation`

Responses:

- `keep`: the treatment supports the task and the evidence explains why.
- `replace`: change structure, copy, hierarchy, behavior, or expression to resolve the identified mismatch.
- `remove`: remove unsupported content or roleless decoration without losing required meaning or access.
- `exception`: preserve an explicitly requested or established style with its source; record any independent usability defect separately. Style exceptions cannot turn false claims or broken/inaccessible actions into passes.

Write findings in observable terms: “The pending-approval reason is clipped at the tested mobile width, preventing the reviewer from reading it,” not “AI-looking,” “lazy,” or “not premium.” Distinguish facts, inference, preference, and untested assumptions. Claims of faster comprehension or improved task success require appropriate measurements; visual inspection establishes neither.

Correct in-scope factual/content, functional, accessibility, and runtime defects and re-run affected checks. For aesthetic refinements, batch corrections after the selected implementation's first review and recheck the profile's required viewports once; two aesthetic review passes bound preference iteration. Representative-candidate comparison is a separate bounded stage. The aesthetic limit never blocks required defect fixes or their re-verification.

If an aesthetic concern remains after that bound, retain it as unresolved and do not claim the requested anti-slop remediation complete. If evidence, permission, or runtime prevents a required fix/check, report the exact limitation and evidence ceiling; continue independent safe work. Neither a technical score nor a structurally valid report can compensate for an unresolved finding. Preserve domain information, action continuity, useful conventions, accessibility, and responsiveness throughout correction.

## Basis and limits

These sources support the individual principles; they do not establish a universal slop detector or prove that a style reveals AI authorship. They are optional provenance, not mandatory browsing or another reference-loading chain:

- [Anthropic: frontend design through Skills](https://claude.com/resources/articles/improving-frontend-design-through-skills): vendor observations about generic generated aesthetics, not a font/color blacklist.
- [NN/g: visual design principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/): hierarchy, scale, grouping, and contrast.
- [GOV.UK: identify user needs](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/plan-manage-content/identify-user-needs/): content tied to users' actual tasks.
- [NN/g: usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/): status visibility, familiar concepts, control, consistency, and error recovery.
- [NN/g: aesthetic-usability effect](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/): perceived attractiveness can conceal usability issues during evaluation.
- [Tuch et al.: complexity and prototypicality](https://research.google/pubs/the-role-of-visual-complexity-and-prototypicality-regarding-first-impression-of-websites-working-towards-understanding-aesthetic-judgments/): first-impression findings do not establish end-to-end usability; familiarity itself is not a defect.
- [W3C: contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [visible focus](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-visible.html), and [status messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html): inspectable access requirements, not proof of authorship or full conformance.
