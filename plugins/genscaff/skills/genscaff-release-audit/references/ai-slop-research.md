# AI Slop Research

Web research updated on 2026-07-21. Use this document to ground the anti-slop process. Do not use it to claim that a visual style proves AI authorship.

## Scope And Definition

There is no validated visual test that can determine whether a web interface was generated by AI. This skill uses `AI slop` as an operational quality label for output that is cheap to produce but expensive to trust or review: generic, conventional, weakly grounded, action-disconnected, misleading, or superficially polished without product substance.

Evaluate output quality, not authorship. A human can produce slop and an AI-assisted team can produce excellent work.

## Research Sources

### AI-Generated Interface Research

- Microsoft Research, `Interrogating Design Homogenization in Web Vibe Coding`: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/interrogating-design-homogenization-in-web-vibe-coding/
  - Describes the risk that ambiguous intent is filled with probabilistic defaults and converges toward average-looking web design; recommends productive friction such as intent articulation and alternative inspection.
  - Operational implication: require a product contract, materially different directions, a pre-code target, and an adversarial review instead of accepting the first plausible generation.
  - Limitation: this is a research analysis of homogenization risk, not a validated detector or a large-scale final-site benchmark.
- Anderson et al., `The Effects of Generative AI on Design Fixation and Divergent Thinking`: https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642919
  - A 60-participant design study found fewer and less diverse ideas in its AI-support condition and observed fixation on provided examples or AI output.
  - Operational implication: direction comparison must be structural, and the reviewer must challenge whether the final work merely polished the first generated concept.
  - Limitation: the short avatar-design task cannot be directly generalized into a universal web-UI score.
- `Homogenization Effects of LLMs on Human Creative Ideation`: https://doi.org/10.1145/3635636.3656204
  - A 36-participant comparative study reported greater semantic similarity across ChatGPT-supported ideas.
  - Operational implication: use distant-domain falsification and product-contract traceability to challenge interchangeable structures.
  - Limitation: this is a general ideation study, not a website-specific quality standard.
- `Can Generative AI Create Accessible Websites?`: https://doi.org/10.1145/3663547.3759755
  - Its limited study of generated shopping pages found many accessibility defects and contextually inconsistent structures.
  - Operational implication: never trust generated semantics or visual affordances; execute every visible control and separate automated from manual accessibility checks.
  - Limitation: the prompt, domain, and sample are narrow, so the reported counts are not universal generation rates.
- Romero et al., `Usable but Conventional: An Empirical Study on the UX of AI-Generated Interface Prototypes`: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15124
  - A blind evaluation with 92 participants found positive pragmatic ratings but neutral or negative hedonic ratings for AI-generated prototypes, including originality and innovation.
  - Operational implication: usability and technical correctness do not prove distinctiveness. Require product-specific content, decisions, and workflow evidence.
  - Limitation: the study evaluates early prototypes and does not define universal visual tells or production-quality thresholds.
- Chen et al., `Rethinking the UI of GenUI: A Tale of Two Designs`: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13843
  - A study with 24 UX designers and product managers compared unstructured depth-first generation with structured input and breadth-first exploration. Structured input surfaced important facets; breadth-first work revealed more possibilities.
  - Operational implication: expand vague prompts into a product contract and compare materially different directions before selecting one.
  - Limitation: this is a 2026 preprint about design-tool workflows, not a finished-interface quality standard.

### User Need, Task, And Service Continuity

- GOV.UK, `Learning about users and their needs`: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs
  - User needs should sound like real users, be based on evidence rather than assumptions, and trace into user stories and acceptance criteria.
  - Operational implication: label assumptions, write a user need, and trace the primary task and explicit constraints into the UI.
- GOV.UK, `Solve a whole problem for users`: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-2-solve-a-whole-problem
  - Services should be scoped around how users think and should join into a coherent journey rather than around a technology, including AI.
  - Operational implication: test the end-to-end task and reject disconnected showcase sections or technology-first IA.
- GOV.UK, `Make the service simple to use`: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-4-make-the-service-simple-to-use
  - A service should help users complete what they need with minimal help and should be tested across the parts and devices users interact with.
  - Operational implication: task walkthroughs are required on desktop and mobile; screenshots alone are insufficient.
- GOV.UK, `Making prototypes`: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/making-prototypes
  - Prototypes are used to explore and test different designs before commitment; realistic code prototypes help test interactions.
  - Operational implication: the pre-code visual target should expose alternatives and the built result should support realistic interaction checks.
- GOV.UK, `Structuring forms`: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure
  - Every requested item should have a reason and each step should focus on one piece of information, decision, or question.
  - Operational implication: remove unjustified fields and overloaded screens; record each decision and consequence.

### Interaction And Information Scent

- Nielsen Norman Group, `10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design`: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/
  - Relevant principles include system-status feedback, real-world language, user control, consistency, error prevention, recognition, and essential-only content.
  - Operational implication: trace the primary action through feedback, result, terminal state, and recovery; remove irrelevant visual weight.
- Nielsen Norman Group, `Information Scent: How Users Decide Where to Go Next`: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/
  - Users estimate destination value from the label, surrounding context, and prior knowledge. Vague labels and generic imagery weaken that estimate.
  - Operational implication: CTA and navigation labels must predict their destinations and use domain language; product media must add decision information.
- W3C WAI, `Understanding SC 2.4.4: Link Purpose (In Context)`: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/link-purpose-in-context.html
  - Link purpose should be determinable from its text or programmatic context, and agreement between the link and destination title improves continuity.
  - Operational implication: inventory visible navigation and action labels, then verify that each accurately predicts its result or destination.
- GOV.UK Design System, `Button`: https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/
  - Button text should describe the action it performs, and multiple primary actions make the next step harder to identify.
  - Operational implication: require one primary control per area and compare its label with observed behavior.
- GOV.UK Design System, `Confirmation pages`: https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/confirmation-pages/
  - Transaction completion should reassure users and explain what happens next, including reference or recovery information when relevant.
  - Operational implication: require an observable terminal state and a next-step or recovery path.
- W3C WAI, `Understanding SC 4.1.3: Status Messages`: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html
  - Important content changes that do not take focus should be programmatically available to assistive technology.
  - Operational implication: action feedback must be visible and semantically communicated, not only animated or color-coded.

### Measurement And Evidence Limits

- Google Research, `Measuring the User Experience on a Large Scale`: https://research.google/pubs/measuring-the-user-experience-on-a-large-scale-user-centered-metrics-for-web-applications/
  - The HEART framework maps product goals to behavioral signals and metrics.
  - Operational implication: define the success outcome and observable task-success signal before choosing metrics. Performance scores are not substitutes for product goals.
- W3C WAI, `Evaluating Web Accessibility Overview`: https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/
  - No evaluation tool alone can determine accessibility; knowledgeable human evaluation remains necessary.
  - Operational implication: automated PASS values cannot be treated as complete evidence, even inside accessibility, and certainly not as product-quality proof.
- W3C WAI, `Selecting Web Accessibility Evaluation Tools`: https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/tools/selecting/
  - Automated tools cannot check every accessibility aspect and can produce inaccurate or misleading results; a tool cannot determine accessibility on its own.
  - Operational implication: record automated audit, keyboard/focus/state inspection, and assistive-technology or human evaluation as separate evidence states. Missing manual work is `NOT TESTED`, not PASS.
- Chrome for Developers, `Introduction to Lighthouse`: https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/overview
  - Lighthouse is an automated audit tool for performance, accessibility, SEO, and related page-quality checks.
  - Operational implication: parse a real Lighthouse artifact as a technical floor. Do not infer originality, product fit, content truth, or task completion from the score.
- Chrome for Developers, `Lighthouse accessibility scoring`: https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/accessibility/scoring
  - The category score is a weighted average of included automated audits; manual audits do not contribute to the score.
  - Operational implication: even a score of 100 cannot replace the manual task, keyboard, focus, feedback, and status-message checks.
- web.dev, `Why lab and field data can be different`: https://web.dev/articles/lab-and-field-data-differences
  - Lab results represent a controlled device, network, and run rather than the range of real-user environments and behaviors.
  - Operational implication: record Lighthouse environment and do not use a single lab run as proof of real-user success.
- GOV.UK, `Using moderated usability testing`: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-moderated-usability-testing
  - Test tasks should have a clear, believable goal and must not reveal the answer or interaction method.
  - Operational implication: when real user testing is available, use task outcomes rather than asking whether the interface looks good. Do not invent universal success-rate thresholds.

### Practitioner Observation

- SmoothUI, `AI Design Slop: Why AI-Generated UI Looks Generic`: https://smoothui.dev/blog/ai-design-slop
  - Describes repeated purple gradients, glass effects, identical card grids, and one-shot generation without a corrective loop.
  - Operational implication: maintain a closed build-review-fix-recheck loop and reject default visual formulas.
  - Limitation: this is practitioner commentary tied to a product, not an independent empirical standard.
- Slopless, `AI UI patterns`: https://www.slopless.design/
- Impeccable, `Slop catalog`: https://impeccable.style/slop/
  - Both practitioner catalogs repeatedly call out purple/blue gradients, glass cards, glow, gradient text, abstract blobs, identical card grids, oversized hero copy, and interchangeable SaaS composition.
  - Operational implication: Genscaff uses these observations to define a deliberately strict visual policy and cluster-based review prompts.
  - Limitation: neither source is a peer-reviewed validity study. These patterns cannot prove AI authorship or independently prove low quality.

### Materials And Contrast Context

- Apple, `Human Interface Guidelines: Materials`: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials
- Microsoft, `Acrylic material`: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/acrylic
- W3C, `Technique G18`: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G18
  - Platform guidance permits constrained material effects but warns about content layering, contrast, readability, transparency reduction, and solid fallbacks. W3C requires contrast across the actual varying background behind text.
  - Operational implication: do not claim that standards universally ban glass or gradients. Genscaff bans them as its own anti-default visual policy and validates source, computed style, and screenshots.

## Evidence-Based Slop Dimensions

### 1. Product Interchangeability

The interface uses a product name and accent color but lacks domain objects, language, data, decisions, constraints, media, or workflow states. It still fits unrelated categories after superficial relabeling.

Required gate: run a two-domain substitution test. Product name, logo, and color do not count as breaking signals.

### 2. Need-To-UI Disconnect

Screens or sections exist because they are common templates, not because they support a stated user need, explicit constraint, trust requirement, or necessary decision.

Required gate: trace `primary-task`, `primary-cta`, explicit constraints, and differentiators to implementation and evidence.

### 3. Action Discontinuity

The CTA has weak information scent, hidden prerequisites, no feedback, the wrong destination, no terminal state, no recovery, or a silent dead end.

Required gate: record and execute the primary causal chain from cue to recovery on desktop and mobile.

### 4. Conventional Pattern Substitution

The result relies on interchangeable hero-copy-card-CTA structures, identical card grids, generic dashboards, generic phone frames, abstract AI imagery, or trend styling without a product reason.

Required gate: compare at least two composition or workflow directions for new work, then choose by product fit. Record concrete domain signals in the selected result.

### 5. Unsupported Content And Trust

The interface uses fake metrics, invented customers, awards, certifications, testimonials, integrations, performance claims, or realistic-looking data without provenance or prototype disclosure.

Required gate: map claims to user, repository, or external evidence. Mark mock data and assumptions honestly.

### 6. State Theater

Controls look functional while key states, transitions, validation, feedback, empty/error behavior, or result handling are missing.

Required gate: inventory primary-task controls, exercise the task, and fail any undisclosed dead end.

### 7. Technical-Floor Substitution

The work uses build success, responsive screenshots, accessibility checks, or Lighthouse scores as the reason the product experience is good.

Required gate: keep technical evidence separate from product-specificity, task, content, and judgment evidence.

### 8. Self-Attestation Without Evidence

The report contains `true`, `pass`, high scores, or vague notes without real artifacts, exact regions, observed behavior, and independent judgment.

Required gate: validate local screenshots and Lighthouse JSON, reject placeholder language, require cross-field invariants, and retain human review.

## Operational Red Flags

- The reviewer cannot identify the product, target user, and primary task from the main surface.
- Product name, logo, or accent color is the only specific signal.
- Copy can be transferred to unrelated products with noun replacement.
- The first viewport shows decoration but no real object, data, content, workflow, or decision.
- The CTA says `Learn more`, `Explore`, `Continue`, or `Submit` without enough context to predict the result.
- Buttons, tabs, filters, nav items, or forms appear usable but are inert or misleading.
- Each section repeats the same badge-title-description-card rhythm.
- Realistic-looking metrics, proof, or claims have no source or disclosure.
- A single screenshot and blanket booleans stand in for interaction and visual review.
- Lighthouse passes while the product remains generic or the primary task cannot complete.

## Required Response To A Slop Finding

1. Mark the exact criterion as failed.
2. Name the visible or behavioral symptom and its evidence.
3. Identify the missing user need, domain signal, decision, action link, state, or provenance.
4. Fix the highest-severity product or task defect before decorative polish.
5. Capture new evidence and repeat the independent review.

No checklist can prove taste or originality. The gate can reduce false confidence by requiring inspectable product and behavior evidence, a validator-owned live browser rerun, and explicit independent judgment. Even then, a local review JSON proves neither who authored it nor that a fresh subagent performed the review; the root agent must verify that provenance against the actual collaboration mailbox.

## Non-Compensating Interpretation

Do not calculate a single `slop score`. Product specificity, action continuity, content integrity, anti-slop visual policy, accessibility, responsive behavior, visual fidelity, and runtime integrity are separate mandatory gates. One failed or untested gate fails the whole result; a high Lighthouse score or polished screenshot cannot add points back.

`AI slop` has no consensus UI-specific measurement standard. Therefore Genscaff's thresholds, including the two-domain five-axis rule and the complete gradient/glass ban, are intentionally strict internal policies. Report `STRUCTURAL_EVIDENCE_INVARIANTS_VERIFIED` only as the outcome of the defined structural and live-browser invariants, and report review provenance as a separate root-agent mailbox check. Neither is scientific proof that the result is non-AI, human-authored, universally original, usable by representative users, or free of every possible form of slop.
