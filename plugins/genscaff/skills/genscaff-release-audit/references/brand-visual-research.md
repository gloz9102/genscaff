# Brand Visual Research

Live visual inspection performed on 2026-07-02 using desktop viewport `1440x1000`.

Use these observations as design principles. Do not copy proprietary assets, trademarks, exact layouts, copy, photography, or brand-identifying compositions.

## Sources Inspected

- Apple: https://www.apple.com/
- Airbnb: https://www.airbnb.com/
- Netflix: https://www.netflix.com/
- Stripe: https://stripe.com/
- Linear: https://linear.app/
- Vercel: https://vercel.com/

## Apple

Observed first viewport: a slim global nav, a region selector bar, centered headline, one product promise, two simple actions, and a large high-quality MacBook product image. The page shows the next product section peeking below the fold.

Use when the product needs calm focus, high perceived quality, and a single hero object.

Design rules:

- Make one object or product surface dominate.
- Keep supporting copy short and literal.
- Use generous vertical space and few competing elements.
- Put primary and secondary actions close to the promise.
- Let asset quality carry the emotion.

## Airbnb

Observed first viewport: the brand and category nav sit above a wide search control, followed by dense horizontal card rows with real property photos, badges, prices, dates, and ratings.

Use when the product is browse-heavy, marketplace-like, inventory-driven, or trust depends on real objects.

Design rules:

- Lead with the primary search/filter action.
- Use real object cards with consistent metadata order.
- Keep density high but predictable.
- Use photos as decision data, not decoration.
- Make repeated content scannable before adding narrative sections.

## Netflix

Observed first viewport: dark full-bleed content collage, strong brand mark, language/sign-in controls, large entertainment promise, email field, red CTA, and a price plan strip. The background media establishes the product before the user reads.

Use when the product is content, entertainment, media, or high-emotion conversion.

Design rules:

- Make content inventory visible immediately.
- Use contrast and overlay to keep text legible.
- Keep CTA hierarchy aggressive and simple.
- Avoid timid layouts when the product promise is immersive.
- Use motion or media only when it reinforces the content.

## Stripe

Observed first viewport: structured nav, direct sales/sign-in actions, large technical business headline, one high-energy color form, two CTAs, proof logos, and the next product section starting below.

Use when the product is infrastructure, fintech, developer tooling, B2B SaaS, or a complex platform that needs clarity and credibility.

Design rules:

- Let precise copy carry the proposition.
- Use color energy as a controlled accent, not background noise.
- Support claims with product categories, logos, or interface proof.
- Make the next step clear for both self-serve and sales-led users.
- Balance large type with dense proof below.

## Linear

Observed first viewport: dark neutral canvas, quiet nav, huge product-development headline, short AI-era positioning, and a real product UI surface with dense issue/activity details and an agent output panel.

Use when the product is operational software, developer tooling, productivity, or AI-assisted work.

Design rules:

- Show the actual workflow surface early.
- Use restrained color and sharp alignment.
- Prefer real labels, statuses, metadata, and activity over decorative illustrations.
- Let density communicate professional utility.
- Keep copy specific to the user's work model.

## Vercel

Observed first viewport: sparse monochrome layout, simple navigation, action pair, large platform headline, central symbolic triangle, short right-side capability statements, and customer proof near the bottom.

Use when the product is a developer platform, infrastructure brand, or abstract technical category that needs confidence and memorability.

Design rules:

- Use one strong symbol or product proof, not many ornaments.
- Leave negative space intentionally.
- Use short capability statements as positioning anchors.
- Keep the palette disciplined and high contrast.
- Put customer or ecosystem proof within the first scroll region.

## Translation Rules

- If building a product site, choose the reference whose structure matches the product category.
- If building an app or tool, prioritize Linear, Stripe, Vercel, or Airbnb patterns over generic marketing hero patterns.
- If the prompt says "like famous brands", do not make a brand collage. Pick one dominant principle and one secondary principle.
- Map each borrowed principle to the target user's task, domain content, and primary action. A visual resemblance without that mapping is costume copying.
- Include at least one same-domain or same-interaction-model reference when live research is available; a famous homepage alone is weak evidence for an unrelated product.
- If the first viewport cannot name or show the product/workflow, fail the design.
- If removing name, logo, and accent color makes the result fit unrelated categories, fail the design.
- If the primary CTA cannot be followed to a product-specific result and recovery path, fail the design.
- If the result only resembles "modern SaaS" without matching a real observed principle above, fail the design.
